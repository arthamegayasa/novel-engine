### Item 6, the test performed

Names stripped, the bible §4 sample lines:

- A: "Ninety seconds. It'll stop on its own. Nobody hold her tongue." / "I don't know what it is. I know what it does, roughly, and when."
- B: "Noted. Put her on my right and keep talking; the yard's loud." / "Forty houses. Four thousand under the span. You're a doctor, you've done this sum before, you did it in a corridor."
- C: "You were going to say ninety seconds. It was eighty." / "Dok. I knew every ventilator on that ward by sound. Hold the cup with the other hand."
- D: "Tent. You said tent. Did that happen, the tent?" / "Your father. The phone. That one happened. I can tell because I didn't like it."
- E: "The wind is off the cliff this morning, Dok, which is why the yard smells of pine; you said, to the chart, that my phrenic nerve was coming back, and it did." / "Kiranya a pilot does not refuse a channel because he dislikes the harbourmaster."
- F: "He casts when the sky permits, Clock. That's your cue to be elsewhere." / "Every Shore-Warden I've served drew someone of his own first. It's a tradition. Nobody wrote it down."
- G: "A span is held or it is not. The rest is accounting."

Result. D (fragments, echoing the last word, "did that happen") and E (one long courteous sentence opening on weather, "kiranya") are instantly separable. B is separable by "Noted" and by numbers delivered as loads. F is separable by stage vocabulary ("cue", "exit"). G is separable by brevity and the refusal to name anyone. A and C are the problem: both are short declaratives built on a count ("Ninety seconds" / "It was eighty"). I can tell them apart only by secondary features: C corrects or completes someone else's sentence and never asks a question; A hedges about herself ("roughly", "I don't know"). Swap A's second line and C's first line and I would not notice. A also shares the lying method's shape with B (both lie with a true number). Three of the seven voices run on counting. Since C narrates ch 15 and 30 in close third with "short declaratives", the interludes are at risk of sounding like the narrator with the pronoun changed.

## PART 2. The six questions

**(a) Arguable or platitude?** Arguable. The bible §1 states both the claim and the counter-claim, and the plan gives the counter-claim real wins: the Warden "wins the exchange on the merits" (outline ch 10) and gives her "the sum, and she cannot answer it" (ch 22). The second clause of the claim ("the people you save do not" carry you) is under-dramatised in the plan: the only place it is tested is ch 18, where the Post debates putting her out, and the row does not say that the voters are stayers she has sat. One sentence in ch 18's obstacle cell would make the clause earn its place.

**(b) Surprising yet inevitable?** The mechanism answer is both: bed 9 (C-001, ch 0), "ma-af" (C-003, ch 2), eight breaths (C-002, ch 1), the lamp (C-006, ch 11), the rain (C-007, ch 12) assemble into a brain-death examination, and the reader who knows the exam will feel it click at ch 27 and nowhere earlier unless they are a clinician, in which case lamp plus ears plus the count gives it away at ch 12. The human answer is guessable far too early. By ch 7 the reader has: a stroke patient described as locked-in for seven months (bible §4.3), who knows the engagement "said at his bed" and not the fellowship "said only in the doctors' room" (C-014). "He heard her" is the only hypothesis that fits C-014; the shade decoy would predict he knows everything she knows. A neurology resident who believes a locked-in patient cannot hear is also not credible: locked-in syndrome is defined by preserved awareness, and the misbelief (bible §1) is one her training contradicts. Then ch 17 shows Sekar repeating what Raras said in an empty tent, which proves on-page that the unconscious hear her in the Eaves, and the narrator spends ch 17 to 24 preferring "the crossing merged us". The reader is seven chapters ahead of her. That can work as Wolfe-mode irony, but only if the plan admits it and writes those chapters as denial the reader is meant to see, and only if Tris's diagnosis is one in which "cannot hear" is a defensible clinical belief (total locked-in with no established code, or a documented unresponsive-wakefulness diagnosis).

**(c) Decoys disproven on-page; deaths advance plot?** Yes on both counts for D1 (Oske bound him; shades cannot be bound; the Post learns she loops, ch 9), D2 (Sekar repeats the tent; nobody else was there; Raras chooses a death, ch 17) and D3 (Hamid clinical, Sekar the father, Tris everything; she goes to the register, ch 24). RH-1 dies at 20 when Tallow reverses her sentence; RH-3 dies at 25 when Tallow shows the ledger; RH-5 dies at 27. M4's two decoys are the exception: they are named in the mystery index and nowhere else, so they currently die by narration moving on, which rule 6 forbids.

**(d) Any arc all Progress and no Payoff?** No. Arc 1 pays M1 and kills D1 at 9; arc 2 pays M2 at 20 with the midpoint at 17; arc 3 pays M3, M4, BIG and the climax. The nearest risk is arc 2, where chapters 10 to 19 are all progress and the single payoff is ch 20; the midpoint at 17 carries enough weight to prevent the arc reading as a ladder.

**(e) Idiolects or narrator-clones?** Idiolects, mostly. Sekar, Hamid, Tallow, the Warden and the Ridgeholder would survive the names-off test in a line-up. Raras and Tris would not reliably survive it against each other (Part 1, item 6). The shared feature is the count delivered flat. The plan already gives Tris two tells the narrator lacks (never asks a question; completes other people's sentences); the fix is to make those tells load-bearing in the interludes and to deny Raras the completion move.

**(f) Where will the reader get bored?** Ch 2 (doctrine dump), ch 15 to 16 (an interlude followed by a rest chapter, two low-pressure chapters in a row with the midpoint waiting at 17), ch 18 (committee), ch 21 to 22 (two Warden conversations back to back). The cadence rule "no three identical hooks" is met, but hook type is not the same as pressure, and the plan has two pairs of adjacent soft chapters inside the arc where regression readers most often quit.

## PART 3. Rulings

**(i) M1 scene-tier exception (payoff 9, plants at 2, 6, 7, 8): YES, acceptable, with two conditions.** The playbook §2 arc tier (20 to 60 chapters) is calibrated for serials of several hundred chapters; in a 31-chapter book no arc-1 payoff can satisfy ≥10, and the alternative (no mid-level mystery in arc 1) would make arc 1 all progress, which item 4 forbids. The distances 1 to 3 are scene tier by the playbook's own table, and the 7-chapter RH-4 plant exceeds scene tier only because it is the world rule the others depend on. Conditions: (1) RH-4's ch 2 line must state all three shade criteria together ("cannot eat, bleed, or be bound") so that ch 8 (eats) and ch 9 (bound) both land on a sentence the reader has seen; (2) rewrite ledger rule 1 to state the exception generally ("payoffs before ch 10 use the scene tier: ≥1 plant ≥5 chapters earlier and the rule of three") instead of logging a one-off, so that future ledgers for short books do not carry the same note.

**(ii) Fixed-ruler schedule (unchanged 0, 6, 13; partial 20; straight 26; full 29): YES, acceptable, with one condition.** The genre doc's "six unchanged" assumes a reset every ten to fifteen chapters across sixty; its purpose is that the reader must feel the ruler as fixed before it breaks. Three unchanged exposures of a two-beat ruler inside a 600-word minute is enough for a pattern, and returning to "straight" at 26 after the partial break at 20 is a good move: it resets the baseline so the full break at 29 is unmistakable. Condition: the "unchanged" at 13 must be true for both beats. Outline ch 13's minute goal is "check the linen room", and the obstacle cell says "the minute is spent" there; if she is in the linen room she does not fix bed 7's lead at 02:47:10 and does not answer Yunus. The minute must be choreographed (alarm, lead, corridor, linen room, back past bed 3, "Belum, Pak", 02:48:04) and the ledger's "unchanged" claim must survive that choreography. The same check applies to ch 20: if she is at bed 3 when the alarm sounds, say whether R1 is kept or also broken; the ledger says only R2 partially breaks.

## PART 4. Originality check

Nothing reads as lifted outright from *A Regressor's Tale of Cultivation*; the names, places and vocabulary (the Eaves, stayers, Posts, the Shoring, Settlings, the Underdraw, grain, green through stone, Grey Post, Mallow Reach, Low Field, Eightbreath, Oske, Tallow, Marit, Ansel) do not match anything I can place. The Indonesian cast names are ordinary and unflagged. Items that resemble published work:

1. **The loop given by someone who loves the hero so the hero can never be lost, and the hero not knowing who gave it.** This is the structural shape of Return by Death in *Re:Zero* (Satella's gift). The book's version is distinct in motive (a patient who was heard) and in exit (a completed brain-death determination), and that specificity should carry ch 27 and 28. Not a borrowing; a familiar shape that the plan must out-specify.
2. **The externalised save file kept and improved by a companion while the hero is away** (the sitting notebook, Tris's handwriting in it at ch 7, two volumes at ch 22, in Sekar's hands at ch 30). This is the ARToC manual device the engine's own regression-genre.md §2 recommends. The costume is new; the technique is inherited. The bible's claim "no techniques are reused" should be softened to "no names, places or beats", or the device acknowledged as a genre convention.
3. **"Deviation"** as the on-page word for sword-sickness is the standard English translation of the xianxia term for qi deviation. The bible bans cultivation loanwords but keeps this one by inheritance; "the split" and "splitting" are already in the outline and are better.
4. **The fixed-date death the loop cannot bypass, and a rift the summoned fall through**, are ARToC-pattern conventions the engine docs name; no specific beat is copied.
5. **Doro** is the name of the immortal in Octavia Butler's *Wild Seed*. Different character, different world; low risk, noted for completeness.
6. The locked-in patient who heard everything echoes *The Diving Bell and the Butterfly* and *Johnny Got His Gun* as a situation, not a beat.

## PART 5. Verdict: `revise`

### REQUIRED fixes

1. **bible §4.3 Pak Tris, §1 "Protagonist misbelief", clues F5.** Make "Pak Tris could not hear me" a defensible belief for a neurology resident. Change the diagnosis to total locked-in syndrome after pontine stroke with no eye-movement code ever established, charted as unresponsive wakefulness, so that bedside examination could not distinguish it and her belief is wrong but clinical. Without this the narrator's credibility breaks at ch 7 and M3 is solved the same chapter.

2. **bible §3.1 row 8 and §5 BIG true answer; clues Mystery index BIG.** The pull criterion ("someone who heard her without her knowing") contradicts Pak Hamid, who is "awake, blink-communicator" and whom "she knew was awake" (§4.3). Restate the criterion as: a soul she spoke to, or near, when it could not answer her. Yunus is excluded because he could answer and did ("Sudah subuh, Dok?"). Recheck the four arrivals against the new wording (Tris, Sekar, Hamid on a ventilator, Dimas in burst suppression all qualify).

3. **clues Mystery index, M4 row and BIG row; outline Clues columns.** (a) Give M4's two decoys a speaker, a plant chapter and an on-page death chapter each, or delete them from the index. Suggested: "the rite failed on her" spoken in ch 2's doctrine lecture and adopted by Raras, killed ch 13 by Tallow's hook lore (C-020 echo); "the Below's living cannot be rooted" spoken by the envoy in ch 18, killed ch 26 with M4. (b) Rewrite D3's text so it explicitly explains the ch 17 tent event (the crossing left a channel between the merged, so Sekar receives what Raras says even unconscious). As written, D3 cannot account for ch 17 and is dead the chapter it is born.

4. **bible §1.2 "Why she regresses" and §5; outline ch 1 and ch 27.** State who the "six others" who land on swords at ch 1 are (hospital dead from the 311? none of them patients or colleagues?), and why the first cast pulls six strangers while each re-throw pulls exactly one patient. Ch 27's mechanism payoff must cover this or a sharp reader will ask in ch 1 and not get an answer.

5. **bible §3.1 Settling row, §5 M2; clues M2 row, C-011, C-012, RC-01.** Specify what distinguishes a cast-Settling from a natural one. Deaths 1 (ch 5) and 3 (ch 19) occur inside Settlings already under way, so "the register's dates match her deaths thrice" is confounded for two of three and proves nothing to a reader or to the Warden. Either the register records a second, unforecast Settling at the Post's span at the hour of each death (Tallow's ledger makes this visible at 20 and 25), or the "match" is restated as the ch 12 case plus two corroborations. Pick one and write it into the rows.

6. **bible §4.2 Shore-Warden "Never stupid"; outline ch 21 and ch 25.** Add one sentence each: why the sealed room is offered only after the third death (suggested: deaths 1 and 2 were accidents, 3 was chosen; he locks up what has started choosing), and what he wants from sending her to the Settling at ch 25 after keeping her alive at 21 (suggested: the great Settling is forecast and the sum says one more cast buys one more seasoned sword; he spends her because the arithmetic now says to). Without a written motive, 25 fails item 7.

7. **outline ch 13 and ch 20 minute cells; clues Ledger notes "Ruler".** Choreograph both ruler beats inside the 60-second minute for ch 13 (the linen-room detour must sit between R1 and R2) and state R1's status at ch 20. The ledger claims "unchanged" at 13 and "partial" at 20; the outline as written contradicts the first and leaves the second undefined.

8. **clues C-012 and C-021.** Add one echo between plant and payoff to each: C-012, a second glance at the Settling register (ch 13 or 14, as gossip about "the newest one"); C-021, the Ridgeholder's ch 18 sentence including "on his right", as the ledger already proposes. Both currently break ledger rule 1.

9. **bible §3.3 The ward; clues C-001.** "Bed 9 empty since March" on a ward that takes paediatric overflow (Dimas, bed 5) is an internal contradiction and makes the plant louder, not quieter. Replace the superstition with a maintenance reason (headwall outlet out of service, repair ticket open since March, the tag never changed) and keep the nurses' joke as the camouflage.
