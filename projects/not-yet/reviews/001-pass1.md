## 5. Reference-author panel

One paragraph per panelist (bible §8). Each ends with: verdict (pass/revise), top three concrete edits with quoted lines, one thing to keep. Panelists must not all say the same thing.

- **Agatha Christie**: Every clue here is a true fact and every piece of misdirection is a wrong inference drawn by the narrator from true facts, which is the Knox rule kept properly: the six swords are real, the count to eight is real, the heading is real, and the reader is wrong only because Raras is. The hidden hand is on stage from his first appearance under an identity that is nothing but a job ("A frontal sort of man. Nothing in the face that was not needed for the job."), and he is given a name only in a line about a cart, which is the right size of line. Two of the plants are correctly followed by a louder beat (the count at 62 is buried under the binding of the six and the draw; the heading at 156 is covered by the Eightbreath line). The one craft error is the opposite of sawing the clue in half: line 168 glues two halves together, "I placed the name. He had counted to eight before he lifted me.", so that a rereader finds the author's hand rather than the narrator's. The register scene is the best fair play in the chapter because its comedy (the swear read "with the same care") is doing the covering. Verdict: revise. Edits: (1) cut "He had counted to eight before he lifted me." at 168; (2) at 156 cut "and the old man let him" so the pre-emption is a fact of order rather than an underlined one, keeping "and closed the book"; (3) at 152 cut "apart" from "wrote mine below the line, apart", because the line already says it and the extra word is a pointer. Keep: "Six set. Six took." followed by her own "Six set. Seven came." The two counts with a different second number are the whole mystery in miniature and nobody will see it until told.

- **Gene Wolfe**: The narrator never states a falsehood. Every misreading is hers and is marked for the attentive reader by a hedge of the right size: "as far as I could tell", "I think", and best of all "I took this for procedure, like reading back a verbal order, and I was right about that at least", which tells the rereader there was something she was wrong about without saying what. The omission at "Bed eight was." is the method working: a sentence that stops, no dash, no ellipsis, and the ledger holds the rest. Where the chapter over-serves the method is the filing refrain. "I filed the noon" (96), "I filed it under that" (168), "I let it be" (96), "I let it go" (164), "I left him to it" (122): five times the narrator tells the reader which facts are provisional. Severian does this once and lets the reader keep the list; told five times, the mechanism is visible and the reader stops doing the work. One more: "He meant the first part for his men." (50) resolves an ambiguity in the Warden's first words that the text should leave standing, since who those words were for is exactly the kind of thing she should get wrong unnoticed. Verdict: revise. Edits: (1) keep one "filed" (the noon) and cut "I filed it under that"; (2) cut "He meant the first part for his men." and let "He said it again to me, the same words" carry the ambiguity; (3) cut "That was the whole of the examination." so the single hedge "as far as I could tell" is the only authorial flag in the paragraph. Keep: "and I was right about that at least."

- **Ernest Hemingway**: The dry register at the painful beat is right and consistent: "Two forty-eight" as an answer to "Are we dead?", the list of beds done twice, "Bed eight was." The chapter states six things the reader could infer and should cut them: "because you do the list"; "and that is a reflex older than medicine"; "which is how I had any sense that time was passing"; "because that is what you do with a row"; "because it was the only text in the place, and because I could not read a letter of it"; and the second "That was the whole of". The ambulance explanation at 50 ("You keep them talking so that you know when they stop.") is stated but it is not inferable and it is the iceberg's visible tip for what she is not saying about her ward, so it stays. Verdict: revise. Edits: (1) cut the six explainers listed; (2) let Rini say the name off the badge so "knew my name" is earned; (3) fix the register's rule in continuity.md (not on the page). Keep: "Bed eight was."

- **Donald Maass**: The emotional modes are mostly "other" (the reader feels what she will not): the list of beds at 34 is pure inventory and the reader supplies the death; "Where is she?" is answered by a page turning; the closing paragraph is a doctor's inventory of what she has ("the blanket, and the hand ..., and the time") rather than what she lost. At the two most painful beats (the list; the sword that stays frost) the prose pulls back, as it should. The risk in this voice is the over-applied version, coldness everywhere, and the chapter avoids it by letting the dryness crack sideways into small competence ("I took the sword off his knees and put it on the ground beside him and nobody stopped me"), which is the third feeling under grief, the relief of being allowed to work, and is better than the first two. - **Shawn Coyne**: Inciting incident: the hand goes through the ice and there is a slit in the air (14 to 18). Crisis: best bad choice, and it is the right kind: refuse the place (insist on the time, the ward, the name she has) or take the place offered (his right, the blanket, a word that is not her name). So I do not read the sword test and the register as two turns; I read turning point at 76 and crisis-to-climax at 144 to 172, which is the correct shape, and the rubric's A2 note is a question of weight not of structure. What the chapter lacks is the crisis on the page: the decision is compressed to one declarative and its reversal, and the reader never sees the first "Clock" she does not answer. Verdict: revise. Edits: (1) let "Clock" be said across the fire once before the end and let her not look up, so the final "and I looked up anyway" is the second time, which is the §8 move (the same short line meaning something different each time); (2) in the round of the six, give her one refusal so the positive is "yes, but": the driver pulls his hands back, or the radiographer answers "both hands" with a question; (3) trim paragraph 18 by a third so the inciting incident reaches the people sooner.
