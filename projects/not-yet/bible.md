# Story Bible: Not Yet

> Single source of truth for the novel. Every chapter, review, and revision reads this first.
> Keep entries terse and factual. Never record a fact here that contradicts a published chapter; fix the chapter or update the bible deliberately and log it in `continuity.md`.

## 0. Seed prompt (verbatim)

A night-shift neurology resident in Jakarta dies when the hospital collapses in an earthquake and wakes in a world of sword immortals, the only member of the rescue team born without a spiritual root. Each time she dies she returns to the minute before the quake. But every return, one more of her former patients has already arrived in that world ahead of her, remembering things she never told them.

### 0.1 Assumptions made from the seed (routine calls, recorded rather than asked)

1. **Working title** "Not Yet" (the narrator's answer, every loop, to a patient who asks whether it is dawn). The seed has no title.
2. **"Rescue team"** is read as the sword world's own term for the summoned: a corps of other-world dead pulled through to hold up a collapsing sky. In-world they are the **Shoring**. The seed's "spiritual root" is rendered in the world's own vocabulary as a **grain** (see §3.2); the prompt's term is never used on the page.
3. **The sword world does not reset when she dies.** Only she returns to Jakarta's minute; the sword world's clock keeps running while she crosses back. This is the single most consequential call. It is what makes "arrived ahead of her" literal (they land while she is away), it keeps relationships accumulating (regression-genre.md §2, mandatory persistent bond), and it makes each death cost time as well as a life.
4. **"The minute before the quake" is exactly one minute, every time, and the quake always kills her.** The minute is the fixed ruler and the quake is the fixed-date death the loop cannot bypass. Jakarta is on the page only inside these minutes and in what people say about it. No flashback scenes.
5. **"Former patients" are the patients on her ward on the night of the quake, plus one from earlier that year.** Not her whole career.
6. **"Remembering things she never told them"** has a mundane true answer (they were unconscious and heard her; see §5) and a structural one (why they come, in what order, and who built the mechanism). Both are decided here before any chapter is written.
7. **Chapter count** is read as 30 numbered chapters plus a prologue (chapter 0), 31 files, following the engine's default workflow (`/novel-run <slug> 0-5` = prologue + five).
8. **Genre tag** `regression`, applied to a tight literary serial, not a long power-fantasy. The ARToC one-clause-per-paragraph mode is **not** adopted (§8). The house voice guide stands unmodified.
9. **Panel** chosen by me (§8), no `--panel` given.
10. **Protagonist is a woman**, as the seed says; third-year neurology resident (PPDS Neurologi), 31, at a fictional Jakarta public teaching hospital.

## 1. Premise

- **One-line pitch**: A night-shift neurology resident dies in a Jakarta earthquake and wakes as the only rootless member of a sword-immortal rescue corps; every time she dies she gets one more minute on her old ward and comes back to find one more of her former patients already there, stronger than her, repeating things she said to them when she believed they could not hear.
- **Core dramatic question** (the question the final chapter answers): Can Raras end the loop that brings her patients through before the sky it lowers falls on all of them, and what does she have to say, and to whom, to end it? Answered in chapters 28 and 29.
- **Controlling idea** (cause and effect): *Attention paid where it cannot be repaid outlasts rescue: the people you speak to in the dark carry you after your death, and the people you save do not.* Its counter-claim, which the antagonist holds and is allowed to be right about: *arithmetic is the only honest form of love; one village against a province is not a dilemma, it is a sum.* The book argues between the two and does not let either win cleanly.
- **Protagonist misbelief (Cron)**: that a patient charted as unresponsive is unresponsive (she has examined them; the chart says so; she trusts the exam), and that attention without the power to act is worth nothing. The first is a clinical belief, defensible at every bedside in the book and wrong at every one. She sheds it on the page (ch 24) and the second only in the last minute (ch 29).
- **Designed irony (M3)**: a reader may suspect "they heard her" from ch 7. That is allowed. The suspense of M3 is not whether they heard but *what she said* and whether she will let herself be told. Chapters 17 to 24 are written as denial the reader is meant to see (Wolfe lens): the narration states nothing false and prefers the merging decoy because the alternative is the May sentence at Tris's bed.
- **Genre, tone, comps**: regression / portal fantasy with a procedural mystery spine. Tone: dry clinical attention over grief; wonder reported as inventory; humour from register clash (ward idiom against sword-hall idiom). Comps by technique only: the fixed-minute reset of a time-loop story, the fair-play decoy structure of a classic detective novel, the unreliable-by-omission first person of a confessional narrator. No published work's names, places, lines, or specific beats are used. Genre conventions the engine itself recommends are used knowingly: the externalised save file (the sitting notebook), the fixed-date death, the tear the summoned fall through, and the loop given as a gift by someone who could not bear to lose the hero (a familiar shape; the book's specificity, a patient who was heard and an exit through a completed brain-death determination, is what must carry it).
- **Target length**: 30 chapters plus prologue, ~2,500 words per chapter (band 2,200 to 2,800; minute chapters may run 1,900 to 2,400 because the minute itself is short).
- **POV and tense**: first-person past, single narrator (Raras), with two marked close-third interludes (ch 15, ch 30), both from Pak Tris.

### 1.2 Regression motive and loop rules (regression-genre.md §9 checklist)

- **Motive**: **Escape.** She wants to stop dying, because each death lowers the sky, kills a village, re-shakes the hospital, and tears one more of her patients out of their deaths into a war. Her goal sentence is stated in ch 21 ("I was going to stop dying"), and stated in a smaller form in the first reset chapter (ch 6: "I was going to find out what it cost").
- **Why she regresses (truth; clues.md mystery index row BIG)**: the Grey Post's rite, the Underdraw, needs a living anchor in the Below: one soul awake and attending to many dying at the moment of a mass death. The Shore-Warden, who is her first brain-death patient (§4.2), re-aimed the rite at her ward and made *her* the hook rather than a catch. A hook is not caught; it is thrown and drawn back. So she lands without a grain (she never touched the sword that roots the Underdrawn), and each time she dies in the Eaves the line is drawn back to the minute of the cast and thrown again. Each throw lowers the sky one Settling and pulls one more soul who was "turned toward" her: someone she spoke to, or near, when it could not answer her.
- **The cast and the re-throw (two parts of one rite; both must be on the page by ch 27)**: The **cast** is the Post's doing. The Post forges swords and sets them at the tear (six, for this cast: what the Grey Post could afford). The hook is thrown; the cast is a net on an instant: it takes the souls that die in the same second as the hook, wherever they are in the building, up to the number of swords set. So the first arrivals (ch 1) are six strangers who died at 02:48:04 on other floors: a security guard, an ER nurse, a radiographer, a canteen cook, an ambulance driver, a visitor. None from her ward; her ward died at 02:48:09 when the fourth floor came down. The **re-throw** is the hook's own fall. When she dies in the Eaves the line draws back to the moment of the cast and falls again, and a re-throw carries one: the soul most turned toward the hook, onto the one sword the Warden has standing at the tear "in case" (he orders one set after each of her deaths; it is the only standing order the Shoring has that nobody explains). He did not know the first re-throw would follow attachment; by the second he understood the order. In ch 27 he says it in two sentences: "The first throw takes the second. The rest take the turned."
- **What ends it**: the hook comes out when the one who holds the line lets go. The Shore-Warden can only let go when he is pronounced dead by the voice that never finished pronouncing him. She completes the brain-death determination she began on 14 March, under the falling sky, in ch 28. This coincides with her last breakthrough (she finally speaks to the unconscious *knowing* he hears). Her fifth death returns her to the minute one last time (the line unwinding, not a cast); nothing is pulled; no Settling falls; she dies in the quake in ch 29 and does not return.
- **The fixed ruler** (two beats seen unchanged before the first break): **R1**: at 02:47:10 the bradycardia alarm on bed 7 sounds; it is a loose lead; she gets up and fixes it (02:47:10 to 02:47:25). **R2**: at 02:47:50 Pak Yunus in bed 3 asks "Sudah subuh, Dok?" and she answers "Belum, Pak." The quake at 02:48:04. Schedule per minute:
  - ch 0: R1 kept, R2 kept (pen, note, alarm, lead, corridor, bed 3, "Belum, Pak", quake).
  - ch 6: R1 kept, R2 kept; the fifteen seconds between the lead and Yunus she spends at bed 8 with Tris's hand.
  - ch 13: R1 kept, R2 kept. Choreography: alarm at :10, lead fixed by :25, two doors past bed 7 to the linen room (:25 to :45; Lia is there), back along the corridor past bed 3 at :50 where Yunus asks and she answers without stopping, quake at :04.
  - ch 20: **R1 broken** (she goes straight from the station to bed 3 at :04; the alarm sounds at :10 and she does not get up; it rings for the rest of the minute), **R2 kept in words** (he asks at :50; "Belum, Pak"; but she is holding his hand when she says it).
  - ch 26: R1 kept, R2 kept (played straight, so the final break can be heard).
  - ch 29: **R1 broken, R2 broken** (she sets the pen down before :10; the alarm rings; she is at bed 3; he asks; she answers differently; the first tremor comes before she has finished).
  - The genre doc's ≥6-unchanged rule cannot be met in a book with six minutes; three unchanged, one partial, one straight, one full break was ruled acceptable by the plan review.
- **Fixed-date death**: 02:48:04, the quake. Stated flat once per minute chapter. Never explained as anything but what it is until ch 20 (it is the Settling's echo) and ch 27 (the first cast was the first quake).
- **Cycle ledger** (four columns): **Resets**: her Eaves body (remade at the tear at age 31, grainless, healthy), her position (she arrives at the tear, a day's walk from the Grey Post, naked of belongings), the Jakarta minute (identical every time). **Carries**: memory, in full; the crossing time (a season, then two, then a year, then a year and a half: bites at ch 6, 13, 20, 26); nothing physical. **Externalised save file**: the **sitting notebook** she keeps in the Eaves from ch 3 (a record of every split she has sat with; Tris keeps it while she is gone and adds to it; she receives it back as a changed object each return). **Persistent relationship or object**: the **Shore-Warden** from ch 1 (he has waited ten Eaves-years for her and remembers every return), and from ch 7 **Pak Tris** (the bond that accumulates). In Jakarta: the **pen** in her hand every minute.
- **Carry-over rules stated when they bite**: non-reset of the Eaves (ch 6: Wenna's grave); crossing time lengthens (ch 13; ch 20); re-binding of the tongue each return lets the binder glimpse more of her (ch 9: the Post learns she loops); the catch stays when the hook is drawn (ch 13 stated, ch 28 bites).
- **Anchor moves** (three): ch 6, the decisive one in the first third: the Eaves did not reset; everything she did is permanent and everything she missed is lost (what became permanent: Wenna's death, her own entry in the register, the Warden's knowledge of her). ch 20: the crossing now costs a year; the return no longer lands her in the same season as her death (what became permanent: Mallow Reach). ch 28/29: the anchor dissolves; the minute is final (what became permanent: the Underdrawn, all of them, and the Eaves without her).
- **Cycle lengths**: 6, 7, 8, 6, 3 chapters. None near the cap.
- **Death as breakthrough**: see clues.md §Cycle-end rows. She has no realm; each death row names what she arrives *knowing* instead, the thing lost, and whether the ordinal formula ("That was the second time I died") is honest. One dishonest formula (ch 23).
- **Reverse irony**: ch 15 (Tris watches her from outside); ch 17 (Sekar learns the loop from Raras's own mouth while unconscious, a route Raras did not choose); ch 30.
- **Longer-lived antagonist**: the Shore-Warden (44 Earth years + 10 Eaves-years before she arrives), Tallow (60 Eaves-years), the Ridgeholder (centuries).
- **Scale plan**: Arc 1 personal (survive, be useful, the uncanny); Arc 2 institutional (the Post, the rite, the cost); Arc 3 personal again at cosmic stakes (the sky, the man, the exam). De-escalation: the climax is a bedside under a falling sky; the last two chapters are a ward and a ward.
- **Final image**: ch 30, not hers (§9).

## 2. The narrator's voice

- **Who**: dr. Raras Wulandari, 31, third-year neurology resident at RSUP Tjipta Mandala, Jakarta. Colleagues and patients call her **Wulan** or **Dok**; nobody at work uses "Raras". Javanese-Jakartan middle class; father a provincial civil servant (died 22 April, stroke; see §4.1); mother alive in Semarang; engagement to an anaesthesiology resident ended in January. Reads English journals, thinks in clinical English with Indonesian at the edges (Pak, Bu, Dok, Mas, Mbak, subuh, jaga, maaf, belum). Vocabulary ceiling: medical and municipal; she has never read a fantasy novel and does not reach for its words.
- **Register**: dry. Attention as a form of care. Reports wonder as inventory. Grief arrives as a list of objects or a dose.
- **Five habits**:
  1. **Counts.** Doses, minutes, beds, breaths, deaths, Settlings, the number of people in a room. Exact numbers. The count is where the feeling is.
  2. **Localises people.** She describes strangers by where the lesion would be ("a left-sided man", "a frontal sort of courage") and feelings as presentations ("I presented with a dry mouth"). This is her figurative domain; it erodes (see below).
  3. **Compares to the ward.** Ventilator alarms, the nurses' station, chlorhexidine, the 02:00 canteen, the Sudirman flyover at shift change. By arc 3 the comparisons have turned Eaves-ward (timber, resin, the Shoring bell); the erosion is logged in continuity.md.
  4. **Reports what she says to people who cannot answer as plain speech.** "I told her the seizure would stop in ninety seconds." No acknowledgement that the listener was post-ictal, sedated, or dying. This is a tic the reader is meant to stop noticing (clue C-004). It is the submerged seven-eighths of the book.
  5. **Stops a sentence before its point** when it approaches her father, the phone call of 19 April, or the thing she said at Tris's bedside. The sentence simply ends. No ellipsis, no dash, a full stop.
- **Two things she never does**: she never describes her own face, body, or clothes (she has not looked at herself in years; other characters supply it); she never uses the words "miracle", "fate", "destiny", or "meant to". She says "the odds", "statistically", or "I don't know why".
- **Speech channels (fixed for the book)**: spoken dialogue in double quotes. Indonesian words in roman, not italic, and never glossed inside dialogue (the narration may gloss once). **Sent speech** (a stayer speaking through blade-resonance, heard in the chest rather than the ear) in ⟨angle brackets⟩. Interior calculation stays in narration, never in single quotes. No other channel.
- **Paragraph mode**: house default (voice-guide rule 15 stands; the one-line paragraph is the once-per-chapter hammer). The ARToC inversion is not declared.
- **Rhetorical questions in narration**: not in this voice. Cap one per 1,000 words, and only when she is quoting someone else's question back.
- **How the voice changes**:
  - ch 0 to 9: counts are clinical units. Comparisons are Jakarta. She narrates what she says to the unconscious freely.
  - ch 10 to 20: counts begin to include deaths and Settlings. Comparisons split. After ch 17 she narrates what she says to the unconscious less, and the reader should feel the thinning.
  - ch 21 to 29: she stops reporting it altogether (an omission the ledger knows: she is now afraid of being heard). Comparisons are Eaves-ward. In ch 29 the habit returns in full, on purpose, and is the chapter.
  - Interludes (ch 15, 30): close third on Tris; short declaratives with no contractions anywhere in the prose, no questions anywhere, and at least one correction of someone else's word per interlude; he supplies what she will not (her face, her hands, the habit seen from outside). The cover test between an interlude paragraph and a Raras paragraph must pass on contractions and on the presence or absence of completion.

## 3. World

The sword world is **the Eaves**: a land beneath a solid grey sky that hangs like the underside of a roof and lowers in sections. The people who hold it up are **stayers** (sword immortals: they *stay* the sky and they *stay*, not aging). Their halls are **Posts**. The vocabulary is carpentry and shoring, not qi and sects; no East Asian loanwords appear in Eaves speech.

### 3.2 Power or progression system

**Grain** (the seed's "spiritual root"): the capacity to carry sword-will. Eaves commoners are mostly grainless. Every Underdrawn arrives grained because the rite roots them on a sword. Raras is the only grainless Underdrawn in the Grey Post's register.

**Seasoning** (cultivation) tiers, with daily-life consequences:

| Tier | Can stay | Daily life |
|---|---|---|
| green | a room's span, for a breath | sleeps little; eats double; bruises purple-black under the sky |
| seasoned | a house, for an hour | stops aging; wounds close in days |
| heartwood | a village, for a day | stops needing food; can send speech |
| ironwood | a province, for a season | stops needing sleep; the body is wood-hard and the voice goes quiet |
| stone | a region, for lifetimes | the Ridgeholder; rarely leaves the Post; speaks in spans and weights |

Time per tier: ordinary Eaves native, ten years to seasoned, a lifetime to heartwood. Underdrawn, faster: the sword that roots them is already seasoned, so they reach seasoned in a year and heartwood in three to five. Raras: never. What can be bought, stolen, inherited: swords, timber, favour, span (a Post's allotted sky). What cannot: grain.

**Sword-sickness** is the book's medicine. On the page it is always **the split** ("she's splitting", "a bad split"); the genre word for it (the one the plan review flagged as a cultivation-translation term) never appears. Eaves treatment: restraint, a wooden bit in the mouth, prayer to the roof. Raras's treatment: no restraint, protect the airway, time it, watch for the prodrome (she learns the Eaves signs: a smell of resin on the breath, the left hand opening), and tell the stayer when to let go. Her only "power" is reading a prodrome four to ninety seconds before anyone else. It is enough to make her the Shoring's **sitter**.

**Hard limits the story never breaks**: no grain for Raras; no return without a death; the quake always kills her; the Eaves dead stay dead; the Eaves do not reset; nothing physical crosses; no one in the Eaves can reach Jakarta.

## 4. Cast

Idiolect fields per voice-guide rule 27: sentence length, two favourite words, two never-words, comparison domain, how they lie.

### 4.1 Protagonist

**dr. Raras Wulandari ("Wulan", "Dok"; Eaves found-name "Clock")**, 31, neurology resident, grainless Underdrawn, sitter of the Shoring.
- **Want**: at first, to be useful; then to stop dying.
- **Need**: to accept that she was heard; to speak to someone knowing they hear.
- **Wound**: 19 April, 23:40: her father phoned, slurring. She was post-call. She said "Tidur aja, Pak" (just sleep). He died of the stroke on 22 April. She has never said this aloud except at bedsides of people she believed could not hear.
- **Lie**: a patient charted as unresponsive, examined as unresponsive, is unresponsive; attention without power to act is worth nothing.
- **Idiolect**: short to medium sentences; favourite words "roughly" (she is never rough; it is a hedge she uses only about herself) and "fine"; never says "miracle" or "sorry" in English (she says "maaf", in Indonesian, and only to patients); compares to the ward; lies by giving a correct number for the wrong quantity.
- **Sample lines**: "Ninety seconds. It'll stop on its own. Nobody hold her tongue." / "I don't know what it is. I know what it does, roughly, and when."
- **What she knows that others don't**: the quake; the ward; who each Underdrawn was. What she doesn't know: what she said.
- **Arc endpoint**: pronounces the Warden; dies in the quake knowing she will be remembered by the people she talked to in the dark.
- **Found-name origin**: her first words at the tear were "Jam berapa?" (what time is it); Oske transcribed it and the Post named her Clock.

### 4.2 Antagonist(s) and hidden antagonist

**The Shore-Warden, found-name "Eightbreath"; in Jakarta, Bagus Priyono**, 44 Earth years, construction foreman, ten Eaves-years in the Eaves before ch 1; heartwood; commands the Shoring. **The hidden hand.**
- **Truth**: Raras's first brain-death determination, 14 March, bed 9, 03:10 to 03:50. Motorcycle crash on the way to a night shift at a site; massive haemorrhage. She did the exam alone with a senior on the phone, talking the whole time: narrating each step, apologising, telling him about herself to keep steady. Every criterion was met. Something heard anyway. His soul, turned toward her voice at the moment the Grey Post was casting toward the Below, was caught singly and arrived not breathing; it breathed on the eighth count. First word in the register: "ma-af", glossed by Oske as a groan. He rose in the Shoring, became Shore-Warden, learned the Underdraw from the Ridgeholder, and re-aimed it: at the ward, at her. He made her the hook so she could never be lost. The casting was the quake. He tells himself the quake would have come anyway (the Post's doctrine says the Below shakes when the sky settles). He has lived ten years as a man who shores other people's roofs and knows exactly what a roof weighs.
- **Want**: the sky held; Raras here, alive, and never lost. **Need**: to be allowed to have died. **Wound**: forty minutes of hearing a stranger decide he was dead while apologising to him; before that, a life of holding up what other people lived under. **Lie**: that arithmetic justifies him (one village a cast, a province saved a year).
- **Right about**: without the Shoring the Grey Post's span falls within a decade and everyone under it dies; her patients were dying and here they walk; nobody asked the Eaves whether they wanted to die for the Below's dead either. He wins the argument in ch 10 and ch 22 on the merits.
- **Wants what she wants**: the dying attended; the ward's people alive.
- **Never stupid**: he knows she loops from ch 9 (Oske tells him). From ch 14 he asks Tris to report what she says in the sitting tent (he is measuring how much of 14 March the others might hold; Tris's answer is always "nothing I had not heard before", which is true). He offers the sealed room only after the **third** death (ch 21) because deaths one and two were accidents of her job and the third was chosen: he locks up what has started choosing. He sends her to sit a Settling at ch 25, knowing she will stay with the sick, because the great Settling is now forecast for the Low Field within two years, the sum says one more re-throw buys one more seasoned sword before it, and Oske has told him she is reading the register: a year and a half of crossing keeps her away from the register until the great Settling is held or lost. He spends her because the arithmetic says to and because it is the only way he knows to keep her.
- **Idiolect**: medium sentences in load-bearing vocabulary (span, bearing, give, dead load, live load); favourite words "noted" and "bearing"; never says "sorry" (he says "noted") and never says "I"-feel anything (he reports what a structure does); compares to roofs and scaffolds; lies by giving a true number without its denominator. **The Ridgeholder**, stone-tier master of the Grey Post, centuries old. The system, not a villain. Taught Eightbreath the rite because the Post needed the Shoring. Two scenes (ch 18, ch 27). Speaks in spans and weights; one sentence at a time; never uses a name. Sample: "A span is held or it is not. The rest is accounting."

### 4.3 Allies (each able to become an obstacle)

**Pak Sutrisno ("Pak Tris"; Eaves found-name "Wulen")**, 58 at death, retired Bahasa Indonesia teacher, bed 8, pontine stroke in December. **Diagnosis as charted**: unresponsive wakefulness. **Diagnosis in fact**: total locked-in syndrome. In the first week there were inconsistent vertical eye movements; a code was attempted twice and failed; an EEG under sedation was read as diffuse slowing; the family declined transfer for further workup. From week three the chart says "UWS, no purposeful response", every exam she did confirmed it, and she stopped waiting for blinks. Seven months. Her belief that he could not hear was clinical, documented, and wrong. Arrives at her first death (ch 5), a season before her return; green at ch 7, seasoned by ch 14, heartwood by ch 24. **The persistent bond.**
- **Heard**: everything. She read him the football, practised presentations on him, told him about the engagement, her father, the phone call, and once, in May, said at his bedside that if it were her she would want to be let go. He agreed with her and could not say so.

- **Idiolect**: short declaratives; finishes other people's sentences; never asks a question (he could not for seven months and the habit held); **no contractions, ever** (a teacher's spoken formality; Raras contracts freely); corrects other people's grammar and word choice mid-conversation; favourite words "exactly" and "precisely"; never says "um" or "maybe"; compares to grammar and classrooms; lies by completing your sentence with the wrong ending and letting you accept it. **In the interludes (ch 15, 30)** the close-third prose keeps all of this: no contractions in narration, no questions anywhere, and at least one sentence that corrects someone's word. Raras is denied the completion move entirely; she never finishes anyone's sentence.

**Sekar Ayuningtyas**, 19, psychology student, bed 7, anti-NMDA-receptor encephalitis, three months of psychosis, catatonia, and dyskinesias (the loose lead on bed 7 is hers). Arrives at her second death (ch 12); green and fast; the Shoring's most frequent sword-sick.

**Pak Hamid Syahbuddin**, 71, retired harbour pilot (Tanjung Priok), bed 10, Guillain-Barré on a ventilator, awake, blink-communicator. Arrives at her third death (ch 19); seasoned by her return (ch 20) because a year passed.

## 5. Mystery architecture

- **The Big Question** (as the reader would phrase it): *Why do her patients arrive ahead of her each time she dies, and how do they know things she never told them?*
  **True answer (stated plainly here and nowhere else)**: They know because they heard her. Every one of them was charted unresponsive, sedated, ventilated, or dissociated when she spoke to them or near them on night shifts, and she spoke freely because they could not answer. The rite that pulls them was built by the first of them: Bagus Priyono, the Shore-Warden, whom she pronounced brain-dead on 14 March while talking to him for forty minutes. He made her the hook of the Underdraw so that she could never be lost. The cast (the Post's net on the instant of her death) took six strangers who died in her second; each of her deaths since draws the line back to the minute of the cast and throws it again, and a re-throw carries one: the next soul that was turned toward her (spoken to, or near, when it could not answer), in the order of how much she said in its hearing. Each throw lowers the sky one Settling on the tear-side and re-shakes the hospital (the quake is one event seen from six throws). They arrive ahead of her because they land at the moment of her death and she spends a season or more crossing back. She is grainless because a hook is not a catch.
- **Decoy answers**, in order, each the most reasonable reading at its stage, each dying on-page in a scene that advances plot:
  1. **They are shades**: the Eaves makes images of people from a stayer's memory, and these are made from hers, which is why they know her secrets (ch 2 establishes shades in one sentence that states all three criteria: a shade cannot eat, bleed, or be bound). **Dies ch 9**: Binder Oske confirms she bound Tris a season ago ("a soul like a locked room with the lights on"); shades cannot be bound. Advances plot: Oske's re-binding of Raras glimpses that she has been here before, and Oske tells the Warden.
  2. **The binder reads every soul and the Warden feeds the new arrivals her secrets** for leverage (true facts: the binder glimpses; the Warden briefs every new Underdrawn about the Clock; from ch 14 he asks Tris what she says in the tent). **Dies ch 17**: Sekar, waking from a split Raras sat through, repeats what Raras said to her in the tent that night, including the father. Nobody else was there; the Warden was at the Low Field. Advances plot: Raras goes to the Warden and demands to know what the binder sees; he says "less than you say aloud"; she does not hear it; she decides to die on purpose to choose who comes next.
  3. **The crossing merged them with her and left a channel**: everyone drawn through the anchor took her memories as they passed, and the channel stays open, so what the anchor says reaches the merged whether they are awake or not. This explains ch 17 (Sekar received the tent speech through the channel) and the Jakarta knowledge (taken in the crossing), and it is the decoy she *wants*, because it means no one was listening at any bed. **Dies ch 24**: merging would give each of them all of her, and it has not: each knows exactly what she said at their bed and nothing she said elsewhere or only thought (Hamid: the phrenic nerve and nothing else; Sekar: the father, said in April at bed 7; Tris: everything, and none of them the Melbourne fellowship or the thing she never said aloud anywhere). A channel from the anchor cannot be bed-shaped. Tris tells her the May sentence and that he agreed with it. Advances plot: she realises the order is the order of hearing, asks who heard her before Tris, and goes to the register.
- **Mid-level mysteries**, one per arc, true answers:
  - **M1 (arc 1)**: *Is Pak Tris real, and why did he arrive when I died?* True: real, bound a season ago, arrived at the tear the day she died. (Payoff ch 9; scene-tier distances, see clues.md note.)
  - **M2 (arc 2)**: *What does each of my deaths cost? Who is killing me?* True: each death is a re-throw; each re-throw is a Settling with a signature (unforecast, tear-side, within the hour of her death); the register carries a "second" entry on the day of death one (ch 5, which she died inside a forecast village Settling), an unforecast tear-side entry the night of death two (ch 12, no Settling near her), and Mallow Reach, unforecast, the night of death three (ch 19, which she died inside a forecast Settling elsewhere). Three signatures, two of them beside a natural one, which is why nobody saw it: the natural one hid it. Nobody is killing her but the sky, and the Warden lets the sky do it. (Payoff ch 20.)
  - **M3 (arc 3, first half)**: *In what order do they come, and what did they hear?* True: the order of how much she said in their hearing while they could not answer; everything she said at their beds and nothing from anywhere else. (Payoff ch 24.)
  - **M4 (arc 3, second half)**: *Why am I the only grainless Underdrawn?* True: she is the hook. Two decoys, each spoken and each killed on the page: (a) "the rite failed on her", spoken in ch 2 by Oske during the doctrine lecture and adopted by Raras; dies ch 13 when Tallow states the hook lore ("a rite doesn't fail; it catches or it doesn't; you're not in the catch column"); (b) "the Below's living cannot be rooted" (she was alive at the cast, unlike the dead), spoken by the envoy in ch 18; dies ch 26 when Tallow says the living root fine, the hook does not, and names what she is. (Payoff ch 26.)
- All clue placement lives in `clues.md`. This section only states the truths.

## 8. Style constraints for this book

- **Chapter length band**: 2,200 to 2,800 words; minute-plus-arrival chapters 1,900 to 2,400; prologue 1,600 to 2,000.
- **Dialogue share band**: 25 to 35 percent. Interludes may run lower.
- **Paragraph mode**: house default (no ARToC inversion).
- **Banned moves specific to this book**:
  - No dream sequences. Sekar's dream-memories are reported speech only.
  - No Jakarta scenes outside the six minutes. The past arrives only as what people say.
  - No East Asian cultivation loanwords in Eaves speech or narration (no qi, dao, sect, dantian, cultivation, immortal as a noun). Carpentry vocabulary only. "Sword immortal" never appears on the page.
  - No villain monologue. The Warden explains nothing he would not explain; the reader assembles.
  - Nobody calls Raras a genius or chosen. Everyone calls her useless and she corrects them with a specific thing she can do.
  - No "miracle", "fate", "destiny", "meant to" in narration.
  - The cultivation-genre translation term for sword-sickness (flagged in the plan review, Part 4 item 3) never appears on the page. The Eaves say "the split"; Raras says "seizure", "the split", or the specific sign.
  - No dramatised flashback to 14 March, 19 April, or the bedside speech of May. These are only ever reported, partially, by the people who heard them.
  - Sent speech only in ⟨angle brackets⟩; never for the Warden until ch 28 (he speaks aloud; the reader should feel the channel open when he can no longer move his mouth).
- **Reference-author panel for crosscheck** (from `engine/style/reference-authors.md`): **Christie** (fair play; three decoys; the hidden hand on stage from ch 1), **Wolfe** (fair unreliability: she states "I told him" and never states a falsehood; the omission is the method), **Hemingway** (omission with the ledger as the submerged mass; the dry register at the painful beat), **Maass** (emotional mode; pull back at the peak; a doctor's inventories), **Coyne** (value shift per chapter in a tight book where every chapter must move). Alternate for cycle-reset chapters: the Korean serial reader (hook honesty; the debt of each death and its repayment).
- **Lint baseline**: `engine/style/baselines/regression-xianxia-webnovel.json` for the regression-genre numbers that still apply (sentence CV, thought verbs, rhetorical questions), with the house targets for adverbs, filter verbs, ellipses, exclamations, and em-dashes (zero).

## 9. Endings

- **Planned final image**: ch 30, close third, Pak Tris, forty Eaves-years on. The Shoring has a timber ward now. A young stayer lies unconscious after a split. Tris, grainless, sits with him and talks: the drip, the shift, the scores of a league in a city that does not exist here, and a doctor who used to talk to him. Dimas, grown, in the doorway. The sky has not settled in forty years, except once, on its own, which nobody explains.
- **Ch 29's image, which answers ch 0's**: 02:47:04, the pen; the alarm on bed 7; she does not get up to fix the lead. She goes to bed 3 and sits. Pak Yunus asks. She answers. The chapter ends on the first tremor, before the second beat of the ruler, and she is talking.
- **The last line's job**: to be a thing said to someone who cannot answer, by the man who could not answer her, in the plainest ward idiom the book has, and to leave open whether anyone hears. The line belongs to Tris. It is not a moral and not her name.
- **What stays unresolved on purpose**: whether she heard him; whether Pak Yunus heard her in the last minute; what Tallow's fire was; why the sky settled once on its own after the rite ended (the Post's doctrine was not entirely a lie, and the book declines to say how much of it was true).
